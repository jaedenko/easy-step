What it does

  • Repairs the mesh: welds vertices, removes degenerate and duplicate triangles, fixes winding, fills small holes.
  • Merges flat, connected triangles into single planar faces, with holes kept as holes.
  • Exports a closed solid as STEP (AP214), which Fusion 360, FreeCAD and SolidWorks open as a body you can cut, combine and measure.
  • Use CAD faces in the viewer to see exactly which regions were merged.

What it does not do

  • Curved surfaces stay faceted. Cylinders, fillets, spheres and freeform shapes remain many small flat faces. Only planes are recognised.
  • It does not rebuild parametric features or a sketch history.
  • It cannot make a solid from a mesh that is badly open; the status above tells you if the repair fell short.
  • Hole filling is simple and can look wrong on large or concave holes.

Before you rely on it

  • Best for mechanical parts with mostly flat faces: brackets, enclosures, blocks. Organic or sculpted models (armor, figures, scans) will export, but mostly as dense triangle faces.
  • Units: STL files carry no units. The STEP is labelled millimetres and numbers are unchanged. If your model was in inches, scale it in your CAD tool.
  • Check the result in your CAD tool before using it for manufacturing, printing or anything safety-related. Verify dimensions and volume.
  • Large files are limited by your browser's memory. Hundreds of thousands of triangles can be slow.
  • Privacy: conversion happens locally on your device. Nothing is uploaded or stored.
